<section class="chapter" id="zones">
  <div class="chapter-head"><span class="chapter-num">01</span><h2>Ocean zones</h2></div>
  <p class="lede">Oceanographers divide the ocean into zones by depth and light, and life changes dramatically from one to the next.</p>
  <p>The <strong>sunlight zone</strong> reaches down to about 200 metres and supports most marine plants and the majority of visible sea life. Below that, the <strong>twilight zone</strong> gets very little light. Beyond about 1,000 metres, the <strong>midnight zone</strong> is in permanent darkness, home to creatures that make their own light through bioluminescence.</p>

<section class="chapter" id="threats">
  <div class="chapter-head"><span class="chapter-num">04</span><h2>Why the ocean matters, and what threatens it</h2></div>
  <p>The ocean produces a large share of the oxygen we breathe, mostly from microscopic phytoplankton, and absorbs a significant portion of the carbon dioxide released into the atmosphere. It also moves heat around the planet through currents, regulating global climate.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Overfishing:</strong> Removing fish faster than populations can recover.</li>
    <li><strong>Plastic pollution:</strong> Waste that breaks down into microplastics found throughout the food chain.</li>
    <li><strong>Warming waters:</strong> Rising ocean temperatures stress coral reefs and shift species' ranges.</li>
    <li><strong>Acidification:</strong> Absorbed carbon dioxide lowers ocean pH, making it harder for shellfish and coral to build their skeletons.</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="callout"><p>Marine protected areas, fishing quotas, and reduced plastic use are among the most widely recommended tools for slowing damage to ocean ecosystems.</p></div>
</section>

<section class="chapter" id="glossary">
  <div class="chapter-head"><span class="chapter-num">05</span><h2>Glossary of ocean terms</h2></div>
  <dl class="glossary">
    <div class="gloss-item"><dt>Plankton</dt><dd>Tiny plants and animals that drift with ocean currents, forming the base of the marine food chain.</dd></div>
    <div class="gloss-item"><dt>Bioluminescence</dt><dd>Light produced by a living organism, common among deep-sea creatures.</dd></div>
    <div class="gloss-item"><dt>Current</dt><dd>A continuous, directed movement of ocean water, driven by wind, temperature, and salinity.</dd></div>
    <div class="gloss-item"><dt>Hydrothermal vent</dt><dd>A fissure on the seafloor that releases geothermally heated water, supporting unique ecosystems.</dd></div>
    <div class="gloss-item"><dt>Continental shelf</dt><dd>The shallow, gently sloping seabed surrounding a continent before it drops to the deep ocean floor.</dd></div>
    <div class="gloss-item"><dt>Salinity</dt><dd>The measure of dissolved salt in ocean water.</dd></div>
  </dl>
</section>
